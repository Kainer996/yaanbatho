## Problem Statement

Eating from the tin is a real, common pattern for nomads, van-dwellers, hostel-hoppers, and anyone living lean. The current ritual is to hold a 400g can of beans/stew over a cheap butane camp stove and wait until it's warm enough to eat. It works, but:

- Holding a hot, increasingly heavy can over an open flame is awkward and unsafe.
- A 73 mm food tin is too narrow for the standard pan support on a £15 butane stove — it tips.
- Direct flame on the can scorches the lacquer/lining and is uneven.
- One hand is always tied up; you can't open another tin, stir, or boil a kettle alongside.

The need: a tiny, cheap accessory kit that sits on the stove someone already owns and turns "hold-it-over-the-flame" into "drop the can in and eat in 3 minutes."

## Design Constraints

- **Cheapest viable.** Target BOM under £15 for the kit, ideally under £10. The user already owns the stove and the tins.
- **Compatible** with any standard single-burner butane camp stove (the Halfords/Argos/supermarket-tier device that takes blue butane cartridges).
- **Packs flat** — fits in a coat pocket or backpack side-pouch.
- **No electronics, no batteries.** The whole point is nomadic resilience.
- **Safer than the status quo.** Stable can position, hands free, less scorching, eating-handle that doesn't burn fingers.

## Solution Overview

A three-part flat-pack kit:

1. **Reducer trivet** — a small (~120–130 mm) wire or enamel ring that drops onto the stove's existing pan support and gives a stable 73 mm-friendly platform for a standard UK food tin. Off-the-shelf: KitchenCraft Gas Ring Reducer Trivet (~£4–£6) or generic universal moka-pot trivet (~£3 for a pair).
2. **Wind/heat shroud** — a folded sheet of stainless or aluminium with cut-outs for ventilation that wraps around the can. Cuts butane consumption ~30–50% in any breeze, evens out the heating, and stops you scorching one side. Packs flat, ~£2–£4 in materials.
3. **Cool-grip can sleeve + spoon** — a silicone or leather wrap with a clip that snaps around the rim of a hot tin so you can hold it and eat from it without burning your hand. Pair with a long-handle camping spoon. ~£3–£5.

Optional v2 add-on: a small folding "claw" stand (three stainless legs + wire cage sized to a 400g tin) that replaces the reducer trivet entirely and gives a dedicated cradle.

## Food-Safety Notes (important)

Heating food in the original tin is fine occasionally but worth being honest about:

- Heat accelerates leaching from the can lining (BPA or BPA-replacement resins) — research has shown roughly an order-of-magnitude faster migration at boiling-water temperatures.
- Direct flame scorching is the worst case: it discolours the lacquer and can release metal compounds.
- The kit's design choices — reducer trivet (no direct flame contact), windshield (even, lower-temp heating) — already mitigate the worst of this.
- The "right" answer for daily use is to decant into a small stainless mess tin. The kit will ship with that as the recommended pattern, with eating-from-the-tin positioned as the convenience mode for occasional use.

Also: butane stoves indoors need real ventilation. Any documentation/packaging will lead with that.

## Build Plan

### Phase 1 — Personal prototype (this week, ~£15)

- Order a KitchenCraft 12 cm gas ring reducer trivet.
- Cut a windshield from a foil oven-tray or a thin steel sheet, three panels hinged with wire.
- Wrap a silicone pot-handle sleeve into a tin-rim grip; test eating from a hot tin.
- Time the heat-up of a 400g tin of beans with and without the windshield. Log fuel use per cartridge.

### Phase 2 — Refined kit (~1–2 months)

- Replace the off-the-shelf trivet with a custom-bent stainless wire cradle sized exactly for a 73 mm UK tin and a 99 mm "big tin" (stew/chilli).
- Design a proper folding 3-panel windshield in 0.3 mm stainless — packs flat, stands rigid.
- Source or sew a leather can-grip with a clip rivet. Leather > silicone for the nomad aesthetic and durability.
- Sleeve the whole kit in a small canvas pouch.

### Phase 3 — Sellable kit (later, only if it earns its keep)

- Small batch: laser-cut stainless windshield + bent wire cradle + leather grip + spoon, all in a flat pouch.
- Photograph the actual nomad use-case (the existing cheap Halfords stove + tin + this kit).
- List on Etsy / a simple Shopify page. £20–£25 RRP, ~£10 BOM at small batch.

## Key Risks / Open Questions

- **Tin diameter variance.** UK 400g tins are roughly 73 mm; bigger stew tins ~99 mm; ravioli tins are different again. The cradle needs to accommodate at least two sizes without rattling.
- **Stove variance.** Different cheap butane stoves have different pan-support geometries. The reducer trivet has to sit stable on most of them. Need to test on at least 3 stoves.
- **Liability.** If we ever sell this, the food-safety and ventilation guidance has to be unambiguous and printed on the packaging.
- **Is the windshield worth it?** May add complexity for marginal benefit if the user is mostly cooking out of the wind. Phase 1 testing decides this.

## Why This Project

It's small, real, cheap, and solves an actual lived problem. It's the opposite of a SaaS pitch — it's a piece of physical kit that makes one ritual nicer for someone living lean. Worth a fortnight of weekends and a £15 BOM.
