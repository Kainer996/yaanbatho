## Make the clearing your own

The personal clearing has twice its previous ground, walkable and decoratable area. Each corresponding radius grows by `sqrt(2)`, preserving old coordinates: ground 16 → approximately 22.63, walking 14.3 → 20.22, decorating 10 → 14.14 world units. This is the player's home, separate from settlement population, jobs, provisions and production.

The decoration catalogue now contains 32 distinct modeled designs, including garden beds, ivy, a pond, arch, picnic furniture, shelves, a settee, wardrobe, spinet, globe and worktables. Crafting spends the player's actual timber; placing, rotating, moving and storing reuse owned items. Each space holds at most 32 placements, with 96 across the home and at most 100 owned copies of each design. Furniture must fit its room or garden, leave fixed furnishings and walking approaches clear, and avoid other solid placements. Rugs remain flat and walkable.

A Cosy cottage costs 25 timber and opens room construction. The Keeper's library costs 28 timber, Sunlit conservatory 34, and Maker's workshop 42. Each purchase opens a physical study doorway, a separate furnished room with its own saved decoration layout and a readable local story. Built rooms also appear in the exterior roofline. The main house's later hearthhome upgrade remains 60 timber. Building a doorway stores conflicting movable study furniture in its existing inventory; it does not destroy ownership. Reserved approaches keep the desk and room doors reachable.

Twenty-eight stable yard trees support three nearby physical strikes, with each strike saved and the third granting three timber once per tree per UTC day. A felled tree becomes a stump and frees its trunk collision. The eight original home discoveries retain their IDs and claims; three room stories bring the home journal to eleven entries. The original flowerpot gift remains one-time.

## Explore villages and towns

Village Walk and the new Town Walk button borrow the settlement's actual scene, camera and renderer. The character frame reads the real player name and level; Satchel reads actual coins, timber and stone. Existing interaction buttons move into one contextual tray with their native handlers intact. Journal tracks the selected request or field story; compass direction derives from the walking camera. WASD/thumbstick moves, drag/arrows looks, E interacts, F enters doors, J opens Journal and B opens Satchel. Back/Escape closes the active panel before leaving a room or walking session. Modal controls stop movement and do not stack over another active panel.

Town discoveries use the founding heart ward's stable seed, so visiting its town cannot reroll or double-claim that ward's request. Individual building doors retain their own ward seed, including valid seed zero. Town animation runs in walking mode: it updates scene life without performing a second render or rebuilding daylight scenery beneath the borrowed camera. Indoors, moved outdoor action buttons and the outdoor tracker remain hidden; native room services retain their existing gates and identities.

Twelve additional field stories provide 36 authored interactions and twelve memories. Three stories are assigned per saved settlement record: find an illustrated clue, prepare a separate object, then choose an answer using the recorded evidence. Wrong answers offer a specific hint without spending resources or advancing progress. Completion pays 8–10 coins and sometimes one existing material, once. The original fifty villager requests, thirty scrolls, request deck order, loot IDs and claims remain unchanged. Full API and content details: [VILLAGE_FIELDWORK_V385.md](VILLAGE_FIELDWORK_V385.md).

Actual village/town trees now fall after the existing three-strike durable claim, then leave stumps. Geometry updates target the existing tree's instance matrices or vertex ranges inside shared scenery batches; neighbouring trees are preserved. The reward remains three timber, or two stone for a rock, per saved object per UTC day. Partial settlement strikes remain session-local; the final receipt is durable. Failed saving grants nothing and leaves the tree available. Presentation errors after commit must report that the supplies were saved, not suggest a second claim.

Outdoor collision rebuilds wait while the player is inside a room. On return or regrowth, a player overlapped by a restored trunk moves to nearby allowed ground with their viewing direction preserved. Reentry refreshes visuals from the existing receipt, not from a new reward roll.

## Persistence, rendering and assets

`player_home_core.js` normalizes `gameState.playerHome` to version 2. Existing introduction, tier, owned items, valid placements, find receipts and next placement ID survive; `rooms` and `trees` extend that record. Pure proposals do not mutate the source home or wallet. The index adapter commits home state and timber together, restores both on failed durable save, and refreshes the display only after success.

Field activity progress lives under the existing village discovery record's `fieldwork`, derived from a fixed `vf01`–`vf12` pool and the original placement seed. Reading old records does not write a migration or reroll content. `villageDiscoveryAdapter` owns the atomic stage/reward transaction; `villageHarvestAdapter` owns actual inventory and daily harvest receipts. None of these systems invents Peeps, homes, employment, food, water or passive income.

The generated [sixteen-object atlas](assets/discoveries-v385/alderwing-objects.webp) supplies request boards, scrolls, treasure, tools, keepsakes and quest objects. [Provenance](assets/discoveries-v385/PROVENANCE.json) records the built-in generation prompt, selected source, lossless WebP encoding and rejected checkerboard variant. Explicit UV bounds follow the selected artwork's irregular packing. Sprites use `transparent:false`, `alphaTest:0.5`, `depthTest:true` and `depthWrite:true`: opaque painted interiors and real depth occlusion, with transparent holes discarded before the manga outline pass. The source texture is shared; failed image loading retains geometry fallbacks, and late callbacks cannot revive a disposed session.

Stand/Sit still moves the existing `#app` DOM onto and off the study monitor. Preserve node/listener identity and both scroll axes, keep the projected app inert while walking, and restore original DOM placement, focus and overflow on exit. No home/room transition may request microphone or camera access or replace a saved real-world walk. Recognition controls continue to require their original native gestures. Home rendering owns one disposable context; settlement walks borrow their existing context. Pause, reduced motion, resize, context loss, late loads and every exit must preserve these ownership boundaries.

## Recovery and release boundaries

If a view fails, use its command-centre/return button or Back/Escape, then reopen it. Graphics failures should leave saved progress intact. A storage failure must be resolved before retrying the exact purchase, find or third strike; do not manually add a reward or clear its receipt. For stale scenery, leave and reenter so visuals reconcile with saved availability. Inspect `BurbzPlayerHome.diagnostics()` and the localhost walking debug state before changing data.

Keep a current exported save before restoring an older runtime. v384's home normalizer does not understand the new rooms, tree records and decoration IDs; reopening and resaving a v2 home with that old code can discard those additions. Prefer a forward correction that retains v2 data. Do not use localStorage/cache clearing as routine repair, and never replace a player's state with a QA fixture.

Keep every changed loader pin aligned with all three worker asset lists and `scripts/update-live-burbz.sh`, including the new HUD, harvest scene, discovery stylesheet and atlas. Shared unchanged dependencies retain their existing pins. Art remains same-origin and never downloads from GitHub during play or deployment. Publish through the existing reviewed merge and guarded updater; verify the deployed build marker, exact managed bytes, worker activation and retained saves. Shipping authorization and the final publication record remain the release owner's responsibility.
